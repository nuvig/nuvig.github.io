reading the archive…

Two clocks, kept apart. A grid, forecast, model, alert or tracker row is stamped when the site captured it, so its time is the arrival. An obs, field, ring, taf or afd row carries only the record's own moment — the observation or issuance time — and was picked up afterwards by the hourly archiver. Those times are drawn with a dotted underline; hover one to see which it is. Sorting the two together and calling the result "arrivals" would be a small lie told a few hundred times a day.

Truncation is stated, never silent. Each line shows the size of the record behind it. One-liners drop the part that carries the least — a METAR's RMK group, a TAF's transmission header, a snapshot's 48 hourly values — and the expansion shows the record whole, or says where it was cut and links the file.

A missing hour is not a quiet one. Each day header prints the hours each METAR stream actually holds, the hours missing, and the hours the station never reported — and marks records healed from IEM after the fact, since a record filled in later is not a record that arrived on time.

The archive is written hourly by the wxarchive Action; tracker snapshots are pushed by the Pi exporter. Pipeline health lives on the changelog; the same days read as weather on the almanac.
