What this is

Lee Airport (KANP) is a single 2,505 ft runway with left traffic on both ends — so nearly every airplane in the pattern flies the same shape: climb, a level downwind, a descending base and final, wheels on. Stagger a day's worth of those nearly-identical phrases in time and you get, literally, a canon. This page plays it.

The voices are real. Tracks come from the site's own ADS-B archive (the same hourly snapshots the flight tracker uses). Altitude picks each voice's note on a Lydian scale, position pans it, distance from the field sets its volume and reverb, groundspeed its brightness. A landing rings a bell. Nothing is quantized in time — every entrance is exactly when it happened.

The room is real too. The wind hiss underneath is that day's archived METARs (KDCA — the nearest archived observations; KANP has no sensor). Rain crackles when it rained; thunder rumbles when storms were reported. The sky brightens and dims on the field's actual sunrise and sunset.

The picture is a long exposure. Every flight burns a permanent trail into the plate, colored by altitude — ember low, ice-blue high. By the end of the day you're looking at the score of the piece you just heard. Hover or tap an aircraft to solo its voice. Positions are ADS-B-equipped aircraft only, interpolated between simplified track points; altitudes are pressure altitude.
