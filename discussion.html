Act IV · The verdict

Did it come true?

Forecast windows close at different times, so each is checked when it closes and the rest stay listed as open — at noon that's last night's low and the morning's ceilings, not a verdict on storms that haven't fired yet. Day rows are the DC forecast against KDCA's METARs; field rows are the KANP hourly grid against KNAK, the field's own sensor (KDCA for days before that stream existed, and for thunder always). Ceilings are checked by height band, not just flight category — a 3,000 ft VFR deck and a clear sky are different forecasts. When something closed and missed, the model's own hindcast says where it went sideways. The ‹ › arrows step back through earlier days, every window closed.

Epilogue · The record

The last 14 days

KDCA's pressure trace and the weather it brought — rain, storms, fog and gusts marked where the METARs recorded them, LWX warnings along the top. Falling pressure means a front or low approaching; rising means high pressure building in. The amber area is hourly GFS CAPE at the field, on its own scale, so a ⚡ over a tall amber peak is fuel spent, and a peak with no ⚡ is a lid that held. All read from this site's own archive.
