The stick-and-rudder skills, from the first takeoff to the checkride tasks.

- Basic Airwork :: The building blocks flown to ACS tolerances.
  - Straight & Level / Trim :: Hold heading and altitude by attitude + power, trimming off the pressure. The reference for everything else.
  - Climbs, Descents & Turns :: Coordinated entries and rollouts, pitch for speed, power for rate, rudder to keep the ball centered.
  - Slow Flight & Stalls {#slow-flight-and-stalls} :: Flight at high AoA near the stall, and power-on/power-off stall recognition and recovery — the feel of the wing running out of margin.
  - Steep Turns :: 45–50° banked level turns holding altitude — a lesson in load factor, back-pressure and coordinated rudder. -> v-speeds-and-the-envelope "loads the wing" -> load-factor "2 g at 60°" -> maneuvering-speed-va "stay below Va"
- Ground Reference :: Flying precise tracks over the ground while the wind tries to blow you off them.
  - Turns Around a Point / S-Turns :: Vary bank to hold a constant-radius track — steepest downwind, shallowest upwind. Pure wind-drift correction. -> wind-correction-triangle "wind drift"
  - Eights on Pylons :: Hold the wingtip pinned on a pylon by flying at pivotal altitude (≈GS²/11.3) — elevator, not aileron, chases the pylon. (See the interactive explainer.) -> groundspeed-and-ld "uses groundspeed"
- Takeoffs & Landings {#takeoffs-and-landings} :: Normal, crosswind, short-field and soft-field variants — the parts of every flight most likely to bite.
  - Crosswind Technique {#crosswind-technique} :: Wing-low slip or crab-to-kick: aileron into the wind, opposite rudder to align with the runway. Limited by control authority and the demonstrated crosswind. -> wind-shear-and-microburst "gusts / shear"
  - Forward & Side Slips {#slips} :: Cross-controlled on purpose: a side slip keeps the fuselage aligned for a crosswind touchdown; a forward slip banks into the wind with opposite rudder to steepen the descent without gaining airspeed — drag as a flight control. -> crosswind-technique "the landing slip" -> parasite-drag "drag on demand"
  - Short & Soft Field :: Short-field: max performance over an obstacle at Vx. Soft-field: weight off the wheels early, in ground effect, never stopping on soft ground. -> ground-effect "uses ground effect" -> region-of-reversed-command "flown behind the curve"
  - Stabilized Approach & Go-Around :: On speed, on glidepath, configured and trimmed by a gate altitude — or go around. The decision that prevents most landing accidents. -> region-of-reversed-command "low-and-slow trap"
- Commercial Maneuvers :: The checkride tasks that prove mastery of energy, not just control — each one deliberately trades airspeed, altitude and bank against each other.
  - Chandelle :: A maximum-performance climbing 180° turn: roll to 30° bank, pitch rising to the 90° point, bank rolling out to the 180° point — arriving wings-level just above a stall with the climb fully spent. Planning, not muscle. -> stall "finishes near the stall" -> load-and-stall-link "bank raises Vs"
  - Lazy Eights :: Two symmetric climbing and descending 180s flown as one flowing figure across a reference line — pitch, bank and airspeed never stop changing, and no two instants match. The purest feel-of-the-airplane maneuver there is. -> adverse-yaw "constant coordination"
  - Steep Spiral :: A gliding, constant-radius spiral around a point through at least three turns, bank up to 60° — an engine-out descent and wind-drift correction rolled into one maneuver. -> emergency-airwork "the engine-out descent" -> load-and-stall-link "steep and slow"
  - Power-Off 180 :: Abeam the touchdown point at pattern altitude, throttle to idle — then land within 200 ft of your spot using only geometry, flaps and, if needed, a slip. Energy management distilled to a single approach. -> slips "slip off the excess" -> groundspeed-and-ld "glide energy"
  - Emergency Descent :: Getting down NOW — for fire, smoke or a failing pilot: power idle, configure as the POH says, bank steeply and hold speed near the applicable limit. The bank keeps positive g and lets you clear the airspace below. -> v-speeds-and-the-envelope "ride the limits"
- Night Operations :: The same airplane in a different world — less to see, more to trust, and your own physiology working against you.
  - Night Takeoff & Landing :: A black-hole final tempts you low — fly the VASI/PAPI and let the landing light (or the attitude, if it fails) shape the round-out. Taxi slower; depth perception is the first thing the dark takes. -> vision-and-night "night vision" -> optical-illusions-on-landing "black-hole illusion"
  - Airport & Aircraft Lighting :: The rotating beacon, blue taxiway and white runway edges, pilot-controlled lighting keyed on the CTAF, and position/anti-collision lights — the code that keeps a dark airport legible. -> non-towered-ops "keying the lights"
  - Night Currency & Equipment :: Three full-stop landings at night in 90 days to carry passengers (night here = 1 hr after sunset to 1 hr before sunrise), and the airplane needs FLAPS on top of the day-VFR list. -> currency-and-recency "the 90-day rule" -> required-equipment-atomatoflames-flaps "the FLAPS list"
- Emergency Airwork {#emergency-airwork} :: Best glide, engine-out field selection and the flows that live in muscle memory. -> groundspeed-and-ld "fly L/Dmax" -> engine-and-power-loss "engine failure"
