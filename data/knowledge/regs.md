The 14 CFR framework — who may fly, in what, when, and how currency and airworthiness are proven.

- PIC Authority & Responsibility (91.3) :: The pilot in command is directly responsible for, and the final authority as to, the operation of the aircraft — and may deviate from any rule as far as an in-flight emergency requires. A written report only if the FAA asks. -> aeronautical-decision-making "authority means deciding"
- Certificates & Ratings :: Student → private → commercial → ATP, plus instrument and category/class ratings. Each unlocks privileges and carries limits.
  - Medical Certificates :: First/second/third class, or BasicMed. The medical you need matches the privileges you exercise.
  - Currency & Recency {#currency-and-recency} :: Flight review every 24 mo; 3 takeoffs/landings in 90 days for passengers (night = full-stop); 6 approaches + holding + tracking in 6 mo for IFR. -> ifr-regulations-and-currency "IFR currency"
  - Pilot Documents & Logbook :: Carry your pilot certificate, medical (or BasicMed paperwork) and government photo ID; the logbook stays home but must prove the currency and endorsements you're exercising.
  - Commercial Privileges & Limits :: The commercial certificate lets you fly for compensation — but carrying persons or property for hire usually also requires a Part 119/121/135 operator behind you. Know the exceptions: instruction, banner tow, ag, aerial photography, sightseeing within the rules.
  - Endorsements :: One-time training endorsements unlock complex (retractable gear + flaps + controllable prop), high-performance (>200 hp), tailwheel and high-altitude (pressurized above FL250) airplanes — logbook ink, not new certificates.
- Airworthiness {#airworthiness} :: The airplane must be in a condition for safe flight and have required inspections done. Pilot and owner share the responsibility.
  - Required Inspections :: Annual (12 mo), 100-hour if for hire, transponder (24 mo), pitot-static (24 mo for IFR), ELT (battery/12 mo), altimeter.
  - Required Equipment (ATOMATOFLAMES / FLAPS) {#required-equipment-atomatoflames-flaps} :: Day-VFR minimum equipment by memory aid; night adds fuses, landing light, anti-collision, position lights, source of power.
  - Documents Aboard (ARROW) :: Airworthiness certificate (displayed), Registration, Radio station license (international only), Operating limitations (POH + placards), Weight & balance data — the airplane's paperwork preflight.
  - MEL & Inoperative Equipment :: With no MEL, an inop item must be required-or-removed/placarded per 91.213 and a pilot/mechanic determination of safety.
- Right-of-Way & Ops Rules :: Who yields (91.113), the 250 KIAS-below-10,000 limit, minimum safe altitudes (91.119), and sterile-cockpit discipline.
- Preflight Action (91.103) :: All available information: weather, runway lengths, performance, alternates, fuel, NOTAMs, and for IFR an alternate plan. "NWKRAFT." -> aeronautical-decision-making "part of good ADM"
- Alcohol & Drugs (91.17) :: Eight hours bottle-to-throttle, under 0.04 BAC, never while impaired or on a disqualifying medication — and no knowingly carrying an obviously intoxicated passenger. The FAA's numbers are floors; judgment is the ceiling. -> imsafe-and-personal-minimums "the IMSAFE self-check"
- Accident & Incident Reporting (NTSB 830) :: Immediate notification for accidents and the listed serious incidents (flight-control failure, crew incapacitation, in-flight fire, turbine failure…), the written report within 10 days — and preserve the wreckage until the NTSB releases it.
